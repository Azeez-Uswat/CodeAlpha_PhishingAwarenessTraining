1. Recognizing Phishing Emails & Fake Websites

Attackers craft psychological lures mimicking reputable entities. Hover or inspect the red-flag markers below to see what makes this lure suspicious:

From: IT Support <support@micros0ft-security-update.com>
To: employee@company.com
Subject: URGENT: Password Expiry Notice (Action Required in 1 Hour)

Dear Valued User,

Your access credentials expire today. To retain access to internal servers and emails, verify your account immediately:

👉 http://login-portal.biz/auth?session=92842

Failure to comply will trigger automated suspension of your active account.

Email Red Flags

  • Mismatched sender domain vs. display name
  • Manufactured urgency or threatening deadlines
  • Generic greetings ("Dear Customer/Employee")
  • Unsolicited attachments (.zip, .iso, .xlsm, .exe)

Website Red Flags

  • Typosquatting domains (e.g., paypa1.com)
  • Misleading subdomains (e.g., paypal.secure-login.com)
  • Lack of valid organizational SSL/TLS cert
  • Missing interactive links and broken footer navigation

2. Social Engineering Psychological Triggers

Social engineering exploits human cognitive heuristics rather than software vulnerabilities:

Authority

Impersonating C-level executives, law enforcement, or IT administrators to suppress questioning.

Urgency & Fear

Imposing artificial deadlines ("account suspended in 15 mins") to trigger panic and hasty clicks.

Curiosity & Greed

Luring targets with bonus distributions, package tracking links, or salacious workplace leaks.

Social Proof

Claiming "all team members have already completed this protocol" to force conformity.

3. Real-World Case Studies

FACC AG BEC Incident ($47M)

An Austrian aerospace parts manufacturer suffered a Business Email Compromise (BEC) when an attacker impersonated the CEO, directing accounting staff to wire funds for a secret expansion project.

Ubiquiti Networks Wire Fraud ($46.7M)

Attackers targeted finance employees through executive impersonation, tricking them into making repeated multimillion-dollar transfers to overseas third-party accounts.

4. Defense-in-Depth Best Practices

  • Inspect Full Headers: Check the return-path, SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment on unexpected communications.
  • Out-of-Band Verification: Verify wire transfer requests or credential changes via known phone numbers or walk-ups, never by replying to the thread.
  • Enforce Strong MFA: Adopt FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware security keys to resist reverse-proxy phishing frameworks (like Evilginx).
  • Hover Before Clicking: Always inspect the destination URL root domain before navigating.
